## Entertainment & Hobbies

### Streaming, Watching, and Listening
When the problem isn't a lack of things to watch but too many options, Netflix Roulette offers a way out: it randomly picks a TV show or movie for you to stream, with filters for director, actors, keywords, or genre so the "random" pick still fits your mood[^1]. Once you've settled on a show, there's the separate question of how big a commitment it is — Tiii.me answers that by taking the name of a TV series and calculating the total time it would take to watch the whole thing from start to finish, letting you gauge whether a binge fits your weekend[^2]. For getting music onto a TV setup, Spoticast fills a gap left by Spotify's lack of native Chromecast support on Android, running in the background and streaming whatever plays in the regular Spotify app over to a Chromecast device for Premium subscribers[^3]. And for a more curated listening session, the Featured Playlist series rounded up a Nintendo-themed collection pulling together orchestral arrangements, 8-bit remixes, and piano solos of music from games like Super Mario Bros. and The Legend of Zelda, compiled by a Spotify user and offered as something to put on while exploring Nintendo's latest hardware[^4].

### Reading and Audiobooks
For readers looking to track and discuss books, Goodreads has long been the default, but it isn't the only option — the site's early simplicity, letting people log books owned, read, or wanted and interact with other readers through reviews and message boards, has inspired alternatives worth considering for the same purpose[^5]. For those who'd rather listen than read, a roundup of audiobooks recommended by Amazon aimed to help fill gaps in areas of life that could use improvement, noting that audiobooks can be absorbed while driving or multitasking, which suits people with busy schedules[^6].

### Photography and DIY Projects
A few entries lean into hands-on hobby projects. Building a portable green screen doesn't require much: with around $40 in PVC pipe and thick green fabric, plus a couple of hours of assembly, you can put together a usable, collapsible green-screen setup for video projects, along with considerations like proper lighting[^7]. For a different kind of build, instructions from World Paper Airplane Association record holder John Collins — "The Paper Airplane Guy" — lay out how to fold the design that, thrown by former arena football quarterback Joe Ayoob, set the world distance record, using nothing more than a single sheet of paper[^8]. On the photography side, a set of tips addresses how to look better in photos based on body type and features, building on more general advice about posing and framing to help people feel less self-conscious in front of the camera[^9]. And when a picture is worth more than words, a Reddit-sourced tip suggests carrying photos or simple drawings — like a toilet with a question mark — to communicate basic needs while traveling in a country where you don't speak the language, an approach well suited to snapping and storing images on a smartphone before a trip[^10].

### Everyday Diversions
Not every diversion is about screens or crafts. A map built by Reddit user atrubetskoy combined hundreds of crowdsourced responses about snow-day thresholds with NOAA's average annual snowfall data to estimate how much snow it typically takes to cancel school in a given area, noting that in some regions like the Midwest and Great Plains, wind chill and temperature can matter more than raw accumulation, and that in certain areas even a snow forecast alone can prompt a closure[^11].
