## Finance & Money

### Taxes
Tax season brings its share of surprises, and not always pleasant ones. When the New York Times published a deep investigative report on the Trump family's tax practices, drawing on more than 100,000 documents including lawsuits, financial paperwork, and depositions, it detailed how the family allegedly set up a sham corporation and used other schemes to help move hundreds of millions of dollars while minimizing tax liability[^1]. On a more everyday level, the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 lowered rates and doubled the standard deduction, leading most filers to expect bigger refunds — but the IRS itself warned that a larger-than-usual share of filers who normally got refunds would instead owe money, and some would face penalties[^2]. Keeping old tax records handy matters too: the IRS's Get Transcript service lets filers download years of past tax return transcripts directly, a major improvement over the previous process, which required a paper questionnaire and a 5-10 business day wait[^3]. Taxes can also factor into unexpected purchases — booking a wedding at a museum, park, or other space owned by a non-profit instead of a traditional banquet hall can cut costs and may even come with a tax deduction[^4]. And for married couples managing student debt, filing taxes as "Married Filing Separately" rather than jointly can sometimes work out better financially, particularly for someone on an Income-Based Repayment plan whose monthly payment is calculated based on individual rather than joint income[^5].

### Student Loans and Debt Repayment
Paying down debt faster than the minimum requires some strategy. One approach for student loan borrowers is to request extra principal-only payments, but that only works well if the loan servicer — companies like Navient, Nelnet, FedLoan, or Great Lakes — is willing to "ungroup" separate loans so a borrower can target extra payments at a specific high-interest loan rather than having them spread across a bundled group[^6]. Geography can matter too: because state income tax rates vary widely, relocating can meaningfully change how much a borrower pays toward their loan over time, as illustrated by a Student Loan Hero calculator and the story of the company's CEO, who moved from New York City to income-tax-free Austin, Texas[^7]. Once a big debt — whether a car loan, student loan, or credit card balance — is finally paid off, the newly freed-up discretionary income calls for a plan, starting with steps to avoid sliding back into debt[^8]. For borrowers whose credit has already taken a hit from missed payments, a "goodwill letter" offers a way to ask a lender or servicer to remove a reported late payment and restore an account to good standing, a tactic typically aimed at those who missed payments due to a temporary financial hardship[^9].

### Credit Scores and Credit Cards
Monitoring and protecting credit standing takes some know-how. Consumers are legally entitled to a free credit report each year via AnnualCreditReport.com, though that service doesn't include an actual credit score; a growing number of companies now offer free access to various credit scores through other channels, though it pays to watch out for credit-score scams[^10]. Just as important as building credit is knowing what can quietly damage it — beyond the obvious issue of missed payments, maxing out available credit hurts the "debt utilization ratio" that credit bureaus use in scoring, and Credit Karma recommends keeping usage under 30 percent of the total limit[^11]. Credit cards can also come with lesser-known perks: some card issuers offer damage protection for cellphones when the monthly phone bill is paid with an eligible card, with Wells Fargo and First Citizens Bank among the more prominent examples out of more than 25 banks and credit unions reviewed[^12]. Card security matters in person, too — travelers can ask a hotel for an "Attestation of Compliance" certificate confirming PCI compliance, the standard of security checks credit card companies require of businesses, since surveys have found some hotel managers unaware they even need to meet the standard[^13]. Credit risk isn't purely individual, either: marrying someone with existing debt can create liability questions, and understanding when a spouse's financial history can affect a joint credit standing is an important step in protecting one's own credit history as a fallback[^14].

### Earning Extra Income
For those looking to boost their income, there's no shortage of paths — from the "super-lazy to the intensely-engaged" — for making extra money in spare time[^15]. An infographic compiling over 200 online resources breaks these options down further, covering freelancing as a writer, designer, or programmer; affiliate and CPA marketing; adding content to network sites; producing videos; and flipping web properties[^16]. Earning more from an existing job is its own skill, and negotiation research suggests that job candidates who set the "anchor" by naming a number first in a salary negotiation tend to come out ahead — and, according to a University of Idaho psychological scientist's research, even framing that number as part of a joke can nudge an employer's ultimate offer higher rather than lower[^17]. When a job doesn't work out and an employer requests a resignation rather than issuing a firing, that moment can double as a negotiating opportunity: since resigning normally disqualifies someone from unemployment benefits, getting the employer to agree in writing not to contest an unemployment claim can preserve that income source[^18]. And for a sense of where personal income stacks up regionally, an analysis using the Pew Charitable Trust's definition of "middle class" — households earning between 67 and 200 percent of a state's median income — combined with U.S. Census data produced state-by-state salary figures showing the middle class had shrunk in every state over the prior decade or so[^19].

### Smart Everyday Spending
Small tactical choices can add up to real savings on everyday purchases. At a restaurant, asking the sommelier for the "bin end list" or "pocket list" can turn up wine that's discounted because the bottle is scuffed or the restaurant is trying to clear out a vintage that's being phased out or is nearly sold through[^20]. Timing matters for bigger purchases as well — airfare tends to be cheapest when booked on Tuesday or Wednesday, and specifically in the early morning hours, such as around 1am, when demand from other shoppers is lowest[^21].

### Retirement and Long-Term Planning
Planning ahead extends beyond day-to-day budgeting. Choosing between a Traditional and a Roth IRA largely comes down to the timing of taxation: contributions to a traditional IRA are tax-deductible up front but distributions are taxed on withdrawal, while Roth contributions aren't deductible but withdrawals of contributions can be accessed differently, a distinction a Charles Schwab infographic lays out for savers weighing the two account types[^22]. Planning for a future where someone may become incapacitated is equally important, and appointing a trusted financial power of attorney agent — someone authorized to manage another person's financial affairs if they're no longer able to — is one of the most common ways people prepare for that possibility, particularly for those aging independently[^23].

### Money and Relationships
Money habits don't always align between partners, and reconciling them can take years. One writer described how she and her partner started out with starkly different spending styles — he tended to spend a work bonus or raise immediately, while she was more conservative — and detailed how the couple worked through the resulting friction to eventually coexist without the fighting that once accompanied their financial differences[^24].

### Consumer Protections
Financial fine print carries consequences beyond the specific product it's attached to. When Dropbox updated its terms of service to include forced arbitration — while also offering a way to opt out — the move drew attention to why forced arbitration clauses are worth avoiding whenever a company provides an opt-out option, a practice increasingly common across various service agreements[^25].
