## Food & Cooking

### Kitchen Techniques & Tricks
A handful of small technique tweaks can make a big difference in the kitchen. When making onion rings, the trick to a satisfying crunch is stirring a bit of instant mashed potato flakes into the tempura-style batter before dredging the onions, which thickens the coating and adds extra snap[^1]. Serving pie cleanly has its own workaround: instead of fighting with the first slice, cut a small "sacrificial" sliver first, which opens up enough room to get a knife or pie server underneath the remaining slices and lift them out intact[^2]. For a campfire dessert, hollow out an orange, fill it partway with cake batter, wrap it in foil, and let it rest on the coals for about half an hour for a single-serving cake baked right inside the peel[^3]. And for cooks looking to vary their weeknight routine, a chart built around 36 world cuisines suggests just three characteristic spices per region, letting something as ordinary as meatloaf take on the flavor profile of Moroccan or other regional cooking[^4].

### Eggs (and Egg Cartons)
Eggs get their own set of workarounds. Boiling a large batch at once risks cracked shells from eggs knocking against each other, but wrapping the eggs in a dish towel—layered between the eggs in the pot—cushions them so they can be boiled in bulk without damage[^5]. Once boiled, peeling can be simplified with nothing more than a teaspoon, maneuvered to pop the shell off in one piece rather than picking at fragments[^6]. Yolks get a second life too: after making cocktails that call for egg whites, the leftover yolks can be cured, then grated over salads, pizza, pasta, or deviled eggs, where the concentrated, savory cured yolk intensifies the classic filling[^7]. Even the carton eggs come in has a use beyond the kitchen—flipped upside down, empty egg cartons make an improvised, if unattractive, laptop stand that lets air circulate underneath a hot laptop instead of trapping heat against your legs[^8].

### Drinks & Bar Tricks
Several tips revolve around drinks and bar culture. Beer Menus is a site (with a mobile-friendly version) that lets beer fans search bars nationwide to see what's on tap or in bottles and cans, along with pricing, after expanding beyond its original New York City coverage[^9]. In the field, a bottle opener isn't strictly necessary: wrapping a hand around the cap and using a spoon's curved handle as leverage against the lip of the bottle can pop the top off in a pinch, similar to the butter-knife method[^10]. Getting a lime wedge into a beer bottle is normally messy, but sliding it in with the flat side of a knife channels the juice into the bottle cleanly instead of leaving it stuck in the neck—a method that requires a steady, careful hand[^11]. Pouring liquids has its own fix, too: turning a juice carton upside down before pouring reduces the glugging and splashing that comes from pouring it right-side up, a trick that also applies to any screw-cap carton, including motor oil[^12]. On the health side, an infographic lays out just how many calories alcohol adds to a diet, noting that a frozen margarita can rival a cheeseburger in calories and a glass of wine can be comparable to a slice of cake, while recommending better mixers like tonic water over sugary ones[^13]. And when liquor stores and grocery delivery slots became hard to come by, local breweries emerged as an alternative source for getting alcohol delivered directly, filling a gap left by regions where grocery and liquor delivery weren't available[^14].

### Comfort Food Favorites
Some entries celebrate favorite comfort foods and creative riffs on them. The burrito gets singled out as a nearly perfect food, its appeal coming from how a simple tortilla wrapped around a mix of regional ingredients can be endlessly varied while still holding together as a cohesive, portable meal[^15]. In a similar spirit of reinvention, a beloved sandwich—the Italian sub, grinder, or hoagie—gets transformed into a hot casserole, turning a cold-cut favorite into a comforting baked dish[^16]. And for a frozen treat with a healthier bent, baking a sweet potato and then freezing it results in a naturally sweet, incidentally vegan popsicle, an idea inspired by a chef's social media posts showcasing the technique[^17].

### Grocery Shopping & Food Science
The last cluster covers the science and logistics around food and shopping. A Consumer Reports chart ranks fish by mercury risk, identifying shellfish like shrimp, scallops, and oysters, along with wild salmon and tilapia, as the safest choices—while also suggesting people eat even less tuna than FDA and EPA guidelines recommend, a concern that's especially relevant for pregnant or nursing women[^18]. Airline food's notorious blandness has a scientific explanation as well: taste and smell both depend on humidity to function well, and the dry, pressurized cabin environment dulls the senses needed to fully enjoy a meal, whether it's the in-flight service or snacks packed from home[^19]. Grocery shopping itself became a challenge worth solving with technology: a web-based notification tool was built to alert shoppers by text when a curbside pickup slot opened up, sparing them from having to repeatedly refresh their cart to snag a delivery window[^20]. And for those unsure what to cook, the Gojee mobile app suggests recipes and drinks based on ingredients already on hand as well as personal likes and dislikes[^21].
