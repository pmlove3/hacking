## Relationships & Social Life

### Dating and Meeting People
Modern dating largely runs through apps now, and the options split along fairly distinct lines: Tinder skews toward casual hookups, eharmony leans intense and marriage-minded, and Hinge is built around personality-driven prompts rather than just photos, so picking the right platform is mostly about matching the app's crowd to what you're actually looking for[^1]. Once a conversation is actually happening, small talk can still be the hard part — comedian Jerry Seinfeld's trick, shared on his web series *Comedians in Cars Getting Coffee*, is to lean on questions with numerical answers ("How long have you lived here?" "What time do you start work?") because they always have a concrete answer and keep the exchange moving[^2]. Before that first date even happens, a bit of online background-checking has become normal practice: part of what makes profile prompts on apps like Hinge useful is that they give you a jumping-off point for small talk, and a reasonable amount of pre-date "stalking" covers basic safety and vibe-checking without crossing into overkill[^3]. And once you're actually dating, physical differences like a big height gap between partners are mostly a non-issue in the grand scheme of things — the main advice is simply not to be weird about it, even though notably short or tall daters will likely run into some stereotyping along the way[^4].

### Sustaining Relationships and Staying Connected
Distance and conflict are two of the biggest pressures relationships face, but neither has to be fatal. A study published in the *Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy*, led by researcher Emma Dargie, found that long-distance couples were often happier than proximate ones, suggesting that raw physical distance doesn't doom a relationship — what matters more is the gap between your expectations for the relationship and the reality of your situation, and getting aligned on that together[^5]. Closer to home, it's worth being able to tell the difference between an ordinary rough patch and a genuinely dysfunctional dynamic: a healthy relationship is supposed to be stable and nurturing, giving both partners a source of support and celebration, so a bad fight is worth examining for whether it reflects a bad day or a deeper, more serious pattern[^6]. For parents, keeping the relationship itself alive can come down to logistics — a "sleepover swap," where you team up with another family and hire one babysitter to watch both sets of kids during a sleepover, splits the cost of date night and makes it easier to actually get out of the house[^7]. And connection doesn't have to be limited to the people already in your life: during the isolation of early lockdown, when Zoom calls and FaceTime covered the basics but travel and new encounters felt out of reach, getting an international pen pal was offered as a way to stay curious about and connected to the wider world[^8].
