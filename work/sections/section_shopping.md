## Shopping & Deals

### Timing Your Purchases
A bit of planning about *when* to buy can save as much money as hunting for coupons. Lifehacker's comprehensive, regularly-updated guide breaks down the best time of year to buy everything from airfare to appliances, based on when stores are clearing old stock, dropping prices ahead of holidays, or simply running seasonal sales[^1]. An earlier version of the same guide covered similar ground, recommending furniture purchases early in the year, laptops in April, and appliances in September to catch off-season, inventory-moving prices[^2].

### Clever Frugal Tricks
Sometimes the fastest way to a lower price is changing what you call the item you're searching for. A Reddit thread on r/Frugal notes that gym mats turn up cheaper when searched as "horse stall mats," and that a medical folding wedge can stand in for a pricier specialty wedge—though commenters caution that prices shift enough that each substitution is worth double-checking before relying on it[^3]. A more physical trick repurposes an old, otherwise-useless gift card as a small spool for a strip of duct tape, so a bit of tape is always on hand for repairs, blisters, or other on-the-go fixes, the same way campers wrap tape around a water bottle[^4].

### Price Matching and Smart Online Shopping
Despite most major retailers having price-match policies, retail analysts found that only about 5% of consumers bother asking a store to match a lower price they've found elsewhere; a roundup of six major retailers' online price-matching policies lays out the specifics so shoppers can actually use that leverage, especially on larger purchases[^5]. For shopping at Amazon specifically, a list of ten favorite tricks includes starting purchases through AmazonSmile, which automatically donates a small percentage of each purchase to a charity of the shopper's choosing[^6].

### Freebies, Coupons, and Rewards
A crowdsourced master list compiled by the blog Couponers United rounds up companies—including brands like Burt's Bees—that will mail out coupons, and sometimes free products, to anyone who simply emails and asks, as long as the email includes a mailing address[^7]. Many retailers and restaurants also hand out freebies, coupons, or discounts around a customer's birthday, and a roundup chart collects the best of these offers from major chains so shoppers know what to claim when their birthday rolls around[^8].

### Grocery Shopping Strategies
Buying dairy in bulk from warehouse clubs like Costco or Sam's Club can cut costs, but smaller households risk letting the extra spoil; the fix is to freeze the excess milk, pouring off enough beforehand to leave room for expansion in the jug[^9]. Thorin Klosowski streamlined his own grocery routine—which he considers a chore—using a mix of automated delivery, simple meal planning, and a handful of in-store tactics to get every trip down to under 30 minutes[^10]. And for shoppers who care about dough-to-ice-cream ratio, a taste test that melted down and strained several brands of cookie dough ice cream identified which pints deliver the most dough per bite[^11].

### Travel Deals
Beyond standard online travel agencies like Expedia, Orbitz, and Travelocity, deal-hunters can find "first dibs" on discounts by following airlines on Twitter, where carriers sometimes share last-minute airfare deals that never make it to the OTAs[^12]. Cruise shoppers have their own seasonal window during the Cruise Lines International Association's annual Cruise Week each October, when lines such as AMA Waterways and Uniworld offer stackable incentives that can be combined with other promotions already on offer, plus extras some travel agents throw in themselves[^13].

### Big Purchases and Buyer Beware
For anyone shopping for a used car, Popular Mechanics put together a two-page checklist that color-codes potential issues from green (not serious) to blue (deal breaker), letting buyers tally the results against a scorecard to judge whether a given car is worth the money[^14]. Gift cards carry their own risks: hidden fees and a patchwork of state and federal rules mean the balance isn't always as reliable as it looks, which is why cash is generally the safer bet for gift-giving[^15]. And before committing to a phone purchase, it's possible to virtualize Android on a PC to try out a device's software experience ahead of buying[^16].
